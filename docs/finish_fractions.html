True Purpose of the Study: This study examined how having access to AI assistance while practicing affects people's ability to solve problems on their own afterwards. We were specifically interested in whether, and when, relying on an AI assistant during learning changes how well people do once the AI is no longer available. In this study, participants solved fractions problems. Depending on the condition you were randomly assigned to, you had an AI assistant on all of the practice problems, on none of them, on alternating groups of three problems, or on one continuous stretch of twelve problems at the beginning or the end of practice. Everyone completed the same final problems without AI assistance.

Why we didn't fully explain this beforehand: Knowing that we were studying the effects of AI assistance might have influenced how much you chose to use the AI assistant or how you approached the problems you solved on your own, which could have affected the validity of our results.

What we measured: Your accuracy, how long you took on each problem, whether you skipped problems, how you used the AI assistant (including the messages you exchanged with it), and whether you left the study window while working.
